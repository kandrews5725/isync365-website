FAQ

Common questions, answered plainly.

Platform support, migrating from what you use today, and pricing details, all in one place.

Will this mix in with my personal contacts?

No. On iPhone, iSync365 creates its own separate contacts account — completely separate from your personal iCloud contacts. It shows up everywhere personal contacts do (Phone, Messages, FaceTime), but it's never merged with anything already on the device. Nothing gets deleted, reorganized, or combined with what's already there. If someone ever loses access to a list, only that separate business account is affected — their personal contacts are never touched.

Does this give the business access to my whole phone?

No — it's contacts only. Setting this up creates a separate account on your phone that handles just the business contact lists you've been assigned. It doesn't touch your email, photos, messages, location, or anything else on the device. If you're ever removed from a list, that separate account is the only thing affected — nothing else on your phone is touched, whether it's your own device or a company one.

Why iPhone, and not every mobile platform?

iSync365's mobile delivery runs on CardDAV, the standard behind separate, named address books. CardDAV is built into iOS natively — no extra app, no extra setup. On Android, it isn't: getting the same result requires installing and configuring a separate third-party app first, which changes the "no MDM, zero-touch" experience we've built the product around.

For business-owned devices, that's why we built and support iSync365 around iPhone first. It even works on personal iPhones that aren't managed by the business — fully unmanaged, with no app to install. We'd rather do iPhone well than every platform partially. Broader device support is something we're evaluating, not something we're ruling out.

We already have a Global Address List — isn't that enough?

Every Microsoft 365 tenant already includes a Global Address List (GAL) — your organization's built-in directory. But having a GAL doesn't mean those contacts show up anywhere useful. GAL entries are searchable — you can look someone up if you already know their name — but they're not a browsable directory you can scroll through, and you can't organize them into lists inside Outlook — no Sales Team folder, no Vendors folder, nothing like that. If you want a whole department's contacts in front of you, you're opening each person one at a time. iSync365 builds the organized lists your GAL can't — you decide who's on each one — and pushes them right into Outlook, not just onto the phone.

Doesn't Outlook's Save Contacts already put these on my phone?

Outlook's "Save Contacts" option only syncs whatever's already sitting in your personal Outlook Contacts folder — the people you've individually emailed or saved — not your organization's actual directory. If your company has eighty people in it, Save Contacts only puts the handful you've personally interacted with onto your phone, not the real thing. iSync365 works differently: you build organized contact lists, however makes sense for your business, and decide exactly who receives which list — real control you don't get from Save Contacts or a native Exchange account, since both just give you whatever's already sitting there.

Is there a minimum number of users?

Yes — a 5-user minimum applies. Beyond that, adding more is simple: add seats directly in the portal whenever you need to, with no need to contact sales or support. (One small terminology note: inside the app itself, you'll see them referred to as "seats" rather than "users" — same thing, just the term used in the interface.)

What happens if we add users mid-month?

Seats added mid-month are prorated at a daily rate and added to the following month's invoice — no need to notify us or adjust anything manually.

Can I try before I commit?

Yes — ask about a 14-day free trial when you request a demo. Full feature access, no credit card required to start. The minimum-term commitment only begins if and when you decide to convert to a paid plan.

What about adding my work email as a native Exchange account on my phone?

You can, and it's free — but it comes with a real limitation: it only pulls contacts from the mailbox's default root folder. EAS support for subfolders is inconsistent across devices — any folders you've built in Outlook to stay organized (a Sales Team folder, a Vendors folder) may not display at all, or may get flattened into one list, depending on the phone. So even if you've done the work of organizing your contacts, your phone may not see any of that structure. On top of that, EAS is a protocol Microsoft is actively retiring, as covered on our Security & Compliance page. iSync365 solves this directly: pushing specific lists into Outlook and onto the phone via CardDAV creates a real, scrollable, organized directory — automatically, for everyone, not one contact at a time.

Why not just keep using a spreadsheet?

A shared spreadsheet is where a lot of companies start, and it's a reasonable place to begin. The trouble shows up as it grows: entries go stale the moment someone changes a number or leaves, nobody remembers to check it, and getting the right data onto someone's actual phone or into Outlook means manually copying it over — if it happens at all. iSync365 replaces that manual copy-paste cycle entirely: build the list once, and it stays current in Outlook and on the phone automatically, without anyone needing to remember to check a shared file.

What happens to someone's contacts when they leave the business?

Every list is assigned to specific people, not blasted out to everyone. When someone joins, you add them to whichever lists match their role, and their device automatically gets exactly what they need — nothing more. When someone leaves, or changes roles, you simply remove them from the list, or remove their account entirely. From that point, there's nothing left for their phone or Outlook to pull, because the contacts were never tied to a folder sitting on the device — they were tied to their account. The next time their device checks in, whatever they'd been receiving comes off automatically. Nothing to track down, nothing to manually delete — and it works exactly the same whether it's a company-managed phone or someone's own personal device.

We don't have an IT department — is this too complicated for us?

Not at all — iSync365 was built with exactly this in mind. Whoever manages your Microsoft 365 today, whether that's you or an office manager, can set this up themselves; it doesn't require ongoing technical maintenance once your lists are built. And for any devices that aren't centrally managed by the business, we walk through setup with you directly during onboarding, so nobody's left guessing on their own.

What about Android devices?

Android doesn't have native CardDAV support the way iOS does, which is why iSync365 is built around iPhone first. That said, our CardDAV server follows an open standard, so Android users can still connect using a third-party app like DAVx⁵ (a paid app on Google Play), using the same manual account setup already available for unmanaged iPhones.

This isn't something iSync365 builds or officially supports end-to-end — it's a third-party app connecting to a standard our server already speaks. If you'd like to try it, we're happy to walk through the setup, but troubleshooting DAVx⁵ itself falls outside what our support team maintains. Curious how the cost compares to alternatives that support Android natively? See our 3-year cost comparison.

Do prices go up per feature or per list?

No. Every feature is included at every user count. We built it this way on purpose — everything you need for contact syncing, and nothing you don't, at one straightforward price. No feature-gating, no upsells, no guessing what a higher tier might unlock.

Is there an annual contract?

Yes — plans require a 1-year minimum term. Billing itself stays monthly and usage-based, so seat changes are reflected automatically without renegotiating anything.

What about calendar sync?

Some tools in this space bundle shared-calendar sync as part of their pitch. Outlook mobile now natively supports toggling visibility for shared and resource calendars — conference rooms, team schedules, and the like — right in the app. That's largely closed the gap calendar-sync add-ons were built to solve.
