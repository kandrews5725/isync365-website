FAQ

Common questions, answered plainly.

Platform support, calendar sync, and pricing details, all in one place.

Why iPhone, and not every mobile platform?

iSync365's mobile delivery runs on CardDAV, the standard behind separate, named address books. CardDAV is built into iOS natively — no extra app, no extra setup. On Android, it isn't: getting the same result requires installing and configuring a separate third-party app first, which changes the "no MDM, zero-touch" experience we've built the product around.

For business-owned devices, that's why we built and support iSync365 around iPhone first. It even works on personal iPhones that aren't managed by the business — fully unmanaged, with no app to install. We'd rather do iPhone well than every platform partially. Broader device support is something we're evaluating, not something we're ruling out.

What about Android devices?

Android doesn't have native CardDAV support the way iOS does, which is why iSync365 is built around iPhone first. That said, our CardDAV server follows an open standard, so Android users can still connect using a third-party app like DAVx⁵ (a paid app on Google Play), using the same manual account setup already available for unmanaged iPhones.

This isn't something iSync365 builds or officially supports end-to-end — it's a third-party app connecting to a standard our server already speaks. If you'd like to try it, we're happy to walk through the setup, but troubleshooting DAVx⁵ itself falls outside what our support team maintains.

Why not just use the Global Address List and Outlook's Save Contacts?

Every Microsoft 365 tenant already includes a Global Address List (GAL) — your organization's built-in directory — and Outlook mobile has a "Save Contacts" option that can push those contacts to your phone's native contacts app. It works, but everyone lands in one flat list, with no folders, categories, or separation by department, vendor, or anything else once they're on the phone.

Manually adding an Exchange account directly on the phone instead of using Outlook does create some separation, since it shows up as its own distinct account. But that requires each person to configure it correctly themselves, and it relies on Exchange ActiveSync (EAS) — a protocol Microsoft is actively retiring, as covered on our Security & Compliance page. iSync365 gives you the separation without either tradeoff: no manual per-device setup, and no protocol on its way out.

What about calendar sync?

Some tools in this space bundle shared-calendar sync as part of their pitch. Outlook mobile now natively supports toggling visibility for shared and resource calendars — conference rooms, team schedules, and the like — right in the app. That's largely closed the gap calendar-sync add-ons were built to solve. iSync365 stays focused on the problem Outlook and your phone still can't solve on their own: getting your company's contact directory onto every device, organized the way your business actually works.

Is there a minimum number of users?

Yes — a 5-user minimum applies.

Do prices go up per feature or per list?

No. Every feature is included at every user count.

What happens if we add users mid-month?

Seats added mid-month are prorated at a daily rate and added to the following month's invoice — no need to notify us or adjust anything manually.

Is there an annual contract?

No — billing is monthly, based on actual usage, with no long-term commitment required.
